## Summary

- **What thinking is for**:
  - Thinking helps organize important information and make better decisions.
  - **Four steps of thinking**:
    1. **Ask**: focus on the clues.
    2. **Search**: use experience, books, the internet, and experts.
    3. **Conclude**: narrow the scope if uncertain; give conclusions time and care.
    4. **Output**: speak, write, and act. Memory is what remains after thinking.
- **When and how to ask questions**:
  - When overloaded, add a specific number, such as “two key points.”
  - When repeating mistakes, ask what I learned and what to avoid.
  - When stuck on a decision, **come up with at least two or three options**.
  - Ask constructive questions instead of getting stuck in “I'm terrible,” “they're bad,” or “what now?”
- **Five ways to support emotions and communication**:
  - **Appreciation**: don't belittle others. **Affiliation**: share information. **Autonomy**: respect decisions. **Status**: mind how you treat people. **Role**: make room for fulfillment.
  - Use questions instead of orders or lectures, leaving time for people to think, especially experienced colleagues or children.

## My Thoughts

### Ask before helping

I agree with asking what someone needs before trying to help

If I give them all the answers and methods straight away, how much room is left for them to think? I want to remember to leave space for their decisions when offering advice

### More than two extremes

When I get stuck between doing something and not doing it, I want to look for another option

Maybe I can try a small part first or decide later. I don't always have to choose all the way immediately

### What can I explain after reading?

I want to check my understanding by seeing whether I can explain it in my own words

If it sounds convincing while reading but I can't explain the point after closing the book, I want to look back and find where I'm stuck

## Next Steps

- [ ] **Ask deliberately**: start reading with a question and practice asking questions throughout the day.
- [ ] **Dig deeper**: explore a learning point through repeated self-questioning.
- [ ] **Review regularly**: return to conclusion notes and revise them over time.
- [ ] **Try the thinking model**: use “ask → search → conclude → output” in learning or everyday decisions.
- [ ] **Reflect before bed**:
  - Ask what I learned today and how I felt.
  - Record three things to keep and three things to adjust next month.
- [ ] **Use it in life**: try guiding children and a partner with questions instead of direct orders or lectures, and give them time to think.
