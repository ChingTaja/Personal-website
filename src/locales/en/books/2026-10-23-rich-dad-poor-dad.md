## Summary

- **Challenge mental laziness**:
  - The book suggests that saying “I can't afford it” may stop the search for solutions, encouraging questions and thinking beyond familiar limits.
  - The author asks readers to notice financial choices made by following the crowd and to understand how fear and desire influence decisions.
- **Core financial principles**:
  - Study how money works and notice how emotions affect its use.
  - “Don't work for money” is used to encourage learning about finances and sources of income alongside a job.
- **Assets and business**:
  - The author emphasizes continuing to learn how money is obtained, used, and kept.
  - The book distinguishes a career from one's own business, encouraging attention to asset-building activities beyond employment.

## My Thoughts

### Ask another question, and first understand what I want

Before saying I can't afford something, I want to ask: do I really want it, or do I just feel like buying it right now?

If it matters, I can consider other ways and how long I need to prepare. I don't have to stop at “can't afford it,” but I don't have to turn every desire into something I must buy either

### What do I want to understand beyond a salary?

This book makes me want to understand possibilities beyond employment income, without dismissing the value of having a job

I can first understand my income, spending, and the breathing room I want, then gradually learn about assets' income sources and risks

“Making money for me” sounds appealing, but if I can't explain how something earns or loses money, I would rather keep learning than rush to buy

## Next Steps

- [ ] **Read a basic finance book**: add *The Four Pillars of Investing* to my reading list, noting unfamiliar concepts and questions to check.
- [ ] **Organize my finances**: list income, fixed expenses, and existing assets, consider the room I want for everyday life, and decide what to learn next.
- [ ] **Explain before buying**: choose an asset to study and describe its income sources, possible losses, and costs in my own words.
- [ ] **Ask one more question instead of saying I can't afford it**: consider why I want something, whether it is worthwhile, and how much time and money I am willing to put toward it.
