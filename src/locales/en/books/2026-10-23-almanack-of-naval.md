## Summary

### See things clearly before deciding what to think

What stays with me is this: don't rush to a conclusion

Sometimes I've already decided whether I like something or whether it's right or wrong before seeing it clearly. I understand acceptance here as acknowledging things as they are before deciding how to respond, without necessarily agreeing

Goals are similar. Direction is useful, but excluding every other possibility because I set a goal feels like a missed opportunity. Alongside the result I want, I can look at my surroundings and see what else is worth exploring

### Before working harder, think about what I'm doing

The book places a lot of weight on judgment. Before spending hours on something, ask why, whether the method fits, and whether there's a way to increase its impact

I understand leverage as increasing results without only adding working hours. But I still need to know what I'm doing; otherwise I could amplify mistakes too

Learning isn't just remembering conclusions. If I can work back from basic principles, I'm more likely to know how to apply an idea in a different situation

That's why mental models come up. Evolution offers a way to think about adaptation, competition, and cooperation. Game theory reminds me that other people's actions affect my choices. Microeconomics and agency problems draw attention to this: the person making a decision for me might not share my interests

I don't have to understand all of these at once. I can build them up gradually and have more ways to think. Charlie Munger's multidisciplinary models borrow tools from different fields. Mathematics and negotiation are also presented as worthwhile skills: one supports reasoning, the other understanding positions and coordinating interests

### Some things only become visible over time

One workout or reading session may not seem to change anything, but doing them over time lets the accumulation emerge

With reading, I prefer letting curiosity lead. Finding something I genuinely want to understand makes me more willing to keep going than treating reading as another task

Surroundings matter too. The people I meet, the things I read, and how I spend each day can gradually shape my choices. Besides telling myself to work hard, it's worth looking at where my time goes

### Do I have to get it to be happy?

The book spends a lot of its discussion of happiness on desire

Wanting something isn't wrong. But if I keep thinking “once I get this, things will be fine,” the finish line may keep moving

The question I want to remember is: do I have to get it to be happy? Or did seeing someone else have it make me feel I should have it too?

Envy is similar. What we see is usually only one part of someone's life. People who look successful have their own difficulties. Remembering that might help me stop comparing my whole life with a fragment of theirs

As for external judgments and results, some are outside my control. What I can return to is my next choice

### Look at what people actually do

I find the description of charisma interesting: making people feel confidence and care at the same time

It isn't just showing yourself well. It also matters whether someone feels respected and understood around you

Understanding people takes more than listening to the values they say they believe in. Look at their actual choices and what matters behind them

### Don't stay in my head all the time

Alongside thinking, the book reminds me to return to the body and the present

Look around, hear sounds, notice smells, tastes, and touch. Not every moment needs analysis. Breathing can also be a place to return attention to, which is why many meditation practices start there

When a thought or feeling appears, notice it without always reacting immediately. A little space might allow a different choice

### A few questions to keep returning to

Some questions don't need a standard answer immediately. I can leave them for a while and return later

Is what I'm doing really what I want?

Am I choosing, or just continuing because I'm used to it?

Will what I'm pursuing bring the happiness I want?

Which skills would I willingly spend a long time learning and carry into different places?

As for life's meaning, perhaps I need to live while looking for my own answer

## My Thoughts

This book helped me notice the immaturity and limitations in my thinking\
Besides the reasoning, my favorite part was the chapter on happiness\
We chase numbers, positions, and good salaries\
For most people, isn't the ultimate goal happiness?\
Happiness being connected to desire rather than positive or negative thoughts\
Gives me another way to look at things\
When a desire leaves me obsessed or in pain\
Maybe I can explore the motivation underneath it
