## Summary

- **Small changes compound**: an almost unnoticeable 1% daily improvement can grow dramatically over time. If a stone shows no change after 100 strikes and splits on the 101st, it was the accumulated effort, not just the last strike, that broke it.
- **Goals versus systems**: a goal is the result you want; a system is the process that gets you there. Long-term thinking is less about a single achievement and more about your direction and a cycle of continuous improvement.
- **Identity-based habits**: connect habits with the person you want to become, rather than only with an outcome you want. For example, ask, “What would a healthy person do?”
- **Four laws of the habit loop**:
  1. **Cue**: make it obvious. Design your surroundings, separate spaces, use triggers, and remove cues for unwanted habits.
  2. **Craving**: make it attractive. Bundle temptations, such as watching a show while cycling, and understand underlying motives such as easing anxiety or seeking approval.
  3. **Response**: make it easy. Use the two-minute rule and starter habits to reduce friction.
  4. **Reward**: make it satisfying. Reinforce behavior with visual tracking or immediate feedback.
- **Keeping it going and improving**:
  - Learn to love boredom and rely on your system even when motivation is absent.
  - Reflect regularly: what went well, what didn't, and what did I learn? This helps reveal blind spots created by automatic behavior.

## My Thoughts

### What kind of person do I want to become?

Instead of setting a numerical target, I want to ask: what kind of person would I like to be? Then I can look for one small thing to do today that moves me that way

Changing the wording probably isn't enough, though. I still have to actually do it

### Make starting a little easier

I want to try putting what I need within reach and moving my phone away when I need to concentrate

At least that way, I don't have to argue with myself before I even begin

### When I can't see results yet

The stone example leaves me wondering: when change isn't visible yet, how do I tell whether I'm building something or need a different method?

I want to track whether I'm keeping at it and look back after a while, rather than judge its usefulness every day

## Next Steps

- [ ] **Reshape my surroundings**: review my home and work spaces. Make good habits easier, such as keeping a full water bottle on the desk, and remove cues for unwanted habits, such as social notifications or a nearby phone.
- [ ] **Try the two-minute rule**: reduce a new habit to an easy starting action, such as opening a book to read one page or putting on running shoes and heading out for five minutes.
- [ ] **Try temptation bundling**: pair something I want to do for fun with something I need to do.
- [ ] **Reflect regularly**: set a review time to ask what went well, what didn't, and what I learned, while checking my identity and core values.
