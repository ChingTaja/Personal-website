### Notes are more than a record of knowledge

Recording, thinking, and rephrasing gradually develop your own understanding. Those notes can then become an article.

The process can be broken down into:

1. Collect material
2. Extract passages and key points
3. Think and comment
4. Explain in your own words and connect with life experience
5. Organize and synthesize
6. Identify shared themes and important ideas
7. Assemble an article

**Point → Example → Conclusion**

### Three note-taking techniques

#### 1. Extract and comment → Try this while reading!

**Useful for:** reflecting after reading books or articles, or watching videos

Steps:

1. Record passages that interest you and note the page or source.
2. Reread and remove material that no longer matters.
3. Write comments, questions, or associations below the passage.
4. Return to the original when writing a reflection, adding to or revising your thoughts.

Example format:

| Item | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Original passage | An important point made by the author |
| Source | Book title and page |
| My understanding | Explain it again in my own words |
| My thoughts | Do I agree? What does it remind me of? |
| Application | Where could this apply in life or work? |

**The passage supplies material; commenting is how you actually digest it.**

#### 2. Question and answer → Try this when writing a summary!

**Useful for:** overall reflections on a book, summaries, or recommendations

Answer three questions after reading:

- **Why read it?** What interested me in this book originally?
- **What did I learn?** How would I summarize it in one sentence?
- **Who would I recommend it to?** Who needs this book, and why?

These questions help extract the most important takeaways from a large amount of reading.

#### 3. A versatile writing framework

**Useful for:** turning scattered notes into a complete article

| Structure | Question to answer |
| --- | --- |
| Point | What do I want to express? What did I learn? |
| Example | What story, evidence, or experience supports my point? |
| Conclusion | Why does this matter? What do I conclude? |

For example, an article about note-taking:

- **Point:** taking notes is learning, not just recording.
- **Example:** explaining something in my own words after reading helps me understand more than simply copying it.
- **Conclusion:** notes should help me form a perspective rather than accumulate information.

### Three changes in mindset

| Traditional approach | New approach | Why? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Think first, then record | Record first, then think | Writing itself helps thinking; clarity need not come first |
| Reread and copy repeatedly | Explain in your own words | Explaining checks whether you really understand |
| Only record knowledge | Develop personally relevant ideas | Connect knowledge with life, work, and experience |

Copying a passage is not worthless. The point is to go beyond copying to explanation, commentary, and application.

### A change in writing: build from the bottom up

**Traditional: top down**

Choose a topic → Write an outline → Fill in content

A common obstacle: expecting a complete structure before the thinking is clear can make it hard to start.

**The article recommends: bottom up**

Record ideas → Group and synthesize → Find a theme → Assemble an article

The benefit is gathering material first and discovering worthwhile themes within your own thoughts.

Writing doesn't always have to start from a blank document. It can begin with a note you've already written.

Once there is enough material, rearrange it, add connecting sentences, and shape it into an article.

### Five things to remember

1. **Keep it simple:** don't let the note-taking system become a burden.
2. **Record first, without waiting for inspiration:** start with existing information.
3. **Comment after extracting:** don't only collect other people's views.
4. **Notes are writing too:** rephrasing organizes your own thoughts.
5. **Collect before synthesizing:** you don't need a topic at the start.

Don't treat notes as the end of reading, but as the start of thinking\
Once recording your understanding and associations becomes a habit\
Writing a reflection becomes arranging thoughts you already have, rather than inventing them from nothing
